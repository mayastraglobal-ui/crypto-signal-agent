SMC/ICT concept

The concept: the Fair Value Gap (FVG).

  • Definition: in three candles in a row, the first candle's high is below the third candle's low (bullish), or the mirror image (bearish). The middle candle moved so fast that it left a "gap" where little trading happened. ICT teaches that price often comes back to fill that gap, and that the return is a good entry.
  • Source: the smart-money-concepts README (source 4) uses the same three-candle definition. Our engine/smc.py uses it too, plus a minimum size of 0.25 ATR (ATR = the average candle size). The README claims no profitability and says it is "for educational purposes only".

What evidence exists (critical view):

  1. None of the sources I could open this week gives an out-of-sample test of FVG entries. The README is a definition, not evidence.
  2. Our own engine tests FVG entries inside S5 and S7 against control twins without the SMC part. This week's lifecycle numbers (BACKTEST): S5-SWEEP-MSS-FVG@1.0 15m had only 1 trade (-1.32R), and S7-SILVER-BULLET@1.0 15m had 0 trades. Both are far too few to judge.
  3. The only SMC card with enough trades for a twin comparison is S8-PDH-PDL-SWEEP (no FVG). It "beats its twin" on 1h but "does not beat its twin" on 30m. It also still loses money on both: 1h -1.41R over 5 trades, and 30m -0.50R over 113 trades in the failing-cells list.
  4. The SMC family in the playbook (BACKTEST): -0.44R over 748 trades in RANGE, -0.67R over 117 in WEAK_BULL and -0.09R over 746 in TRANSITION. It has not made money in any regime so far.

Why retail traders fail with FVGs:

  1. Gaps are everywhere on low timeframes. If you enter every one, most are noise.
  2. Traders enter without the higher-timeframe direction (HTF bias), so they buy gaps inside a downtrend.
  3. On 5m / 15m, fees and slippage are a large share of a tight stop. This week's loss case shows the same thing for mean reversion.
  4. Traders draw the gap after the move, where the story looks perfect. Our engine only uses gaps logged at the candle where they became known.

What to do: keep FVG as a hypothesis. Do not trade gaps by eye. Let S5 / S7 collect trades against their twins.

How traders lose money

Case study: fee blindness on low timeframes, and backtests that assume perfect fills.

  1. The mechanism: a trade's cost in R is (fees + slippage + funding) divided by the stop distance. A tight stop on 15m makes the same fee a much bigger share of the risk.
  2. External source: the freqtrade backtesting docs (source 5) say its backtests include fees, but assume every order fills at the requested price with no slippage while the price is inside the candle. A backtest like that looks better than real trading. The docs warn that a backtest "will never replace" a dry run. Our engine does charge slippage and funding, so it is stricter.
  3. Our own numbers (BACKTEST): the fees_slippage loss tag is systematic in 3 tests (rsi2_dip_buy 1h, 30m and 15m). rsi2_dip_buy 15m lost -0.34R per trade over 2181 trades, and only 5% of its losses later reached TP1 anyway. It did not lose because the stop was too tight. The edge was too small for its costs.
  4. Testable rule: mean-reversion entries should only be taken when the stop is wide compared with the price, so that costs are a small share of the risk. This is written as Hypothesis: cost share filter for mean reversion in memory/experiments.md. It is blocked until someone checks that the building blocks can express "ATR as a share of price". I did not write a card for it.
