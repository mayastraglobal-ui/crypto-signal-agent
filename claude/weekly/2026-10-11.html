SOURCE → CLAIM (source 1, peer-reviewed):

  1. Bitcoin trades 24 hours a day, so the authors used trading volume to mark the start and end of a "trading day".
  2. The first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return, in the same direction. This is called "intraday momentum".
  3. The effect is strongest when the first session has the highest volume or volatility, and in market downturns.

SMC/ICT concept

The concept: the fair value gap (FVG).

  • The ICT claim: in three candles, if the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap, the gap between them is an "imbalance". Price "must" come back to fill it, and the fill is an entry in the trend's direction. S5-SWEEP-MSS-FVG and S6-OB-FVG use it.

What evidence exists (critical view):

  1. How a gap forms is well explained. Source 2 (50 US stocks) shows that short-term price changes come mainly from order flow imbalance: more aggressive buying than selling at the best prices, or the other way around. The effect is linear, and bigger when the order book is thin. A gap is simply what fast one-sided flow leaves on the chart.
  2. The "price must return" part has no support there. The paper explains the move while it happens. It says nothing about price coming back later, or about the return being a good entry.
  3. Our BACKTEST numbers cannot judge it yet:
  4. S6-OB-FVG 15m: 0 trades.
  5. Its control twin without the SMC part (S6-OB-FVG-noSMC 15m): FAILED, 95 trades, -0.147R.
  6. S5-SWEEP-MSS-FVG: 12 trades at -0.839R (15m) and 9 trades at -0.653R (30m). Its twin without SMC: 29 trades at +0.074R and 13 trades at -0.023R.
  7. The fact sheet says "too few trades vs its twin" for the -5M versions.
  8. No source I opened tests FVG entries out of sample in crypto.

Why retail traders fail with it:

  1. Gaps appear on every timeframe, so there is always one to justify a trade.
  2. In a strong trend, many gaps are never filled.
  3. The stop goes just past the gap, which is exactly where a normal pullback reaches.
  4. On 5m-15m the stop is tight, so fees take a large share of R.

What to do: do not trade gaps by eye. The engine keeps the FVG cards in testing against their twins.

How traders lose money

Case study: day trading for a living (source 3).

  1. The study covers every person who started day trading Brazilian equity futures in 2013-2015 and kept going for at least 300 days.
  2. 97% of them lost money, and only 0.4% earned more than a bank teller (US$54 a day).
  3. The best trader earned US$310 a day, with a standard deviation of US$2,560. That is a huge risk for a small income.
  4. The authors found no evidence that traders learn and improve over time.

The same pattern in our BACKTEST numbers: the shorter the timeframe and the more trades, the bigger the loss.

  • liquidity_sweep_reversal 5m: 3258 trades, -0.72R per trade.
  • ema_9_21_cross 5m: 1659 trades, -0.49R per trade.
  • P03-FIB-PULLBACK-V5 15m: 10470 trades, -0.28R per trade.
  • PB-B-SWEEP 5m and PB-B-SWEEP-15M failed as "not cost-viable": fees plus slippage were 0.59R and 0.44R per trade.

Testable risk rule: cap trades at 1 per coin per day on cards below 1h, and compare with the same card without the cap.

  • If the cap raises the average R after costs on unseen data, frequency is part of the loss.
  • If it does not, the entries themselves are the problem.
  • This needs an engine option, so it is for the operator; I wrote no card.
