Curriculum

  • Today's item: C10, "Simple Technical Trading Rules and the Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns", William Brock, Josef Lakonishok, Blake LeBaron. Journal of Finance 47(5), December 1992, pages 1731-1764 (per RePEc). The plan's reference matches.
  • Opened: only the abstract, on RePEc (https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html), and the abstract of the follow-up by Sullivan, Timmermann and White (Journal of Finance 1999). The full texts are paywalled; the author's copy gave 403.
  • What the abstract says, in simple words:
  • Moving-average and range-breakout rules on the Dow Jones index (1897 to 1986) gave results that random price models cannot explain.
  • Buy signals were followed by higher and calmer returns than sell signals.
  • What I could not check: whether the effect survived costs and later years. The follow-up's abstract says it re-tests these rules with a correction for data-snooping, but does not give the result, so I cite none.
  • What it means for us: this is the classic evidence for the rule families behind ema_9_21_cross, macd_trend_cross and donchian_breakout. Our own backtests agree only for the slow versions: 4h breakouts hold up, while 5m to 1h crosses lose after costs.
