I did not read the book, so the evidence class is CLAIM. The year (2006) was not on the page.
  • What the review says, in simple words:
  • If you test many rules and keep the best one, its backtest looks better than its future, because luck hides among real effects. That is "data mining bias".
  • The bias grows with more rules and more outliers. It shrinks with longer tests and with rules that are similar to each other.
  • Test against detrended data, so a buy-only rule gets no credit just because the market rose. Also avoid look-ahead and include costs.
  • In the book's test, 6,402 simple rules on the S&P 500 were tried, and none was significant after the correction.

  • It repeats C04 and C05. Our trials counter already raises the bar with every test.
