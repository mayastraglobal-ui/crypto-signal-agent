Curriculum

  • Today's item: C08, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing", Brian Hurst, Yao Hua Ooi, Lasse Heje Pedersen. The Journal of Portfolio Management 44(1), Fall 2017. The plan's reference matches.
  • Opened: the article page and the journal PDF on AQR's site (https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf). The NYU link guess gave 404.
  • What it says, in simple words:
  • Following trends (buying what went up and selling what went down over 1, 3 and 12 months) in 67 markets made money in every decade from 1880 to 2016, after estimated costs and fees.
  • It did best in extreme years, both big rises and big falls, including most deep bear markets. The authors call this the "smile".
  • It lost when markets had no clear trend, or when many markets reversed sharply at once. Its drawdowns reached about 25% and lasted a long time.
  • The authors run trend-following funds (AQR), so they are not neutral.
  • What it means for us:
  • This is the family behind our only passing cells, the 4h breakouts.
  • The paper also predicts bad results in choppy, trendless markets, and that is the market we have now.
  • The paper's trades last months; ours last days. It supports the idea family, not our exact cards.
  • Record: [C08] A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing (Hurst, Ooi, Pedersen 2017) in memory/research_sources.md.
