Curriculum

  • Today's item: C07, "Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity", Markus K. Brunnermeier and Lasse Heje Pedersen. Review of Financial Studies 22(6), 2009, pages 2201-2238 (per the NBER page). The plan's reference matches.
  • Opened: https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/liquidity.pdf (the RFS advance-access version) and https://www.nber.org/papers/w12939.
  • What it says, in simple words:
  • Traders who buy and sell for others need money to do it: their own capital plus a margin (a deposit) on every position.
  • When prices fall fast, margins go up. Traders must sell to meet them, which pushes prices down more and raises margins again. That is the margin spiral.
  • Traders who hold big positions lose money in the fall and must sell more. That is the loss spiral.
  • The two spirals feed each other. So liquidity can disappear suddenly, in many assets at the same time, and prices can overshoot.
  • What it means for us:
  • This is the mechanism behind crypto liquidation cascades. Futures exchanges close leveraged positions automatically, and open interest shows it.
  • It fits the 2026-10-08 sell-off (ten coins at the same hours), followed by sharp falls in open interest on 2026-10-09 (ETH -10.6%, XRP -11.1%).
  • It is a theory, not a measured trading edge.
