Curriculum

  • Today's item: C07, "Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity", Markus K. Brunnermeier and Lasse Heje Pedersen. Review of Financial Studies 22(6), 2009, pages 2201-2238 (per the NBER page). The plan's reference matches.
  • Opened: https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/liquidity.pdf (the RFS advance-access version) and https://www.nber.org/papers/w12939.
  • What it says, in simple words:

1. Traders who buy and sell for others need money to do it: their own capital plus a margin (a deposit) on every position.

2. When prices fall fast, margins go up. Traders must sell to meet them, which pushes prices down more and raises margins again. That is the margin spiral.

3. Traders who hold big positions lose money in the fall and must sell more. That is the loss spiral.

4. The two spirals feed each other. So liquidity can disappear suddenly, in many assets at the same time, and prices can overshoot.

  • What it means for us:

1. This is the mechanism behind crypto liquidation cascades. Futures exchanges close leveraged positions automatically, and open interest shows it.

2. It fits the 2026-10-08 sell-off (ten coins at the same hours), followed by sharp falls in open interest on 2026-10-09 (ETH -10.6%, XRP -11.1%).

3. It is a theory, not a measured trading edge.
