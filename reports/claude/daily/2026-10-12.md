## Curriculum
- Today's item: **C09, "Foundations of Technical Analysis"**, Andrew W. Lo, Harry Mamaysky, Jiang Wang. Journal of Finance 55(4), 2000, pages 1705-1765 (per the NBER page). The plan's reference matches.
- Opened: https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613 and the working paper PDF. The MIT copy gave 404.
- What it says, in simple words:
  1. Chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, double bottoms and 8 others) can be found by a fixed computer rule instead of by eye.
  2. On US stocks from 1962 to 1996, prices behaved differently after a pattern than on normal days. That held for 7 of 10 patterns on NYSE/AMEX and all 10 on Nasdaq; with a stricter test, 5 of 10 on NYSE/AMEX.
  3. "Informativeness does not guarantee a profitable trading strategy." A pattern can carry information that is too small to pay for costs.
- What it means for us:
  1. This is the right first test for our SMC and candle patterns: does price behave differently after the pattern than after a random candle?
  2. Our engine already does this with `reports/feature_evidence.json` and with the "noSMC" control twins every SMC card must beat.
  3. The paper says nothing yet about crypto 15m charts, where our costs alone are 0.25R to 0.31R per trade on several cards this week.
