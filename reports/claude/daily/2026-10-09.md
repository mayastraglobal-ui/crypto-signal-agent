## Curriculum
- Today's item: **C06, "Trading and Arbitrage in Cryptocurrency Markets"**, Igor Makarov and Antoinette Schoar. Journal of Financial Economics 135(2), 2020, pages 293-319. The plan's title, authors and journal match.
- Opened: the authors' accepted manuscript on LSE Research Online (https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409). The NBER number I first tried (w24567) belongs to a different paper, and the LSE personal page gave 404.
- What it says, in simple words:
  1. The same bitcoin can cost a different price on different exchanges, and the gap can last.
  2. Between countries the gaps were large. From December 2017 to early February 2018, Korea averaged more than 15% above the US (40% on some days, the "Kimchi premium"), Japan about 10% and Europe about 3%. Within one country, gaps usually stayed below 1% on average.
  3. Gaps open up when bitcoin rises fast. Capital controls stop traders from closing them.
  4. Net buying pressure that is common to all exchanges explains 80% of bitcoin's price moves. That is in the same period, which is not the same as predicting the next one.
  5. Data: 34 exchanges in 19 countries, mostly January 2017 to February 2018.
- What it means for us:
  1. Our candles come from Binance, with OKX as a fallback. Two big exchanges are usually close, but in a fast market a switch between them could put a small fake jump into a candle series and trigger a breakout or a stop.
  2. The 80% result does not mean our taker_ratio block can predict anything.
