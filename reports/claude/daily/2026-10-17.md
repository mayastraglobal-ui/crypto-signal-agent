- What the review says, in simple words:
  1. If you test many rules and keep the best one, its backtest looks better than its future, because luck hides among real effects. That is "data mining bias".
  2. The bias grows with more rules and more outliers. It shrinks with longer tests and with rules that are similar to each other.
  3. Test against **detrended** data, so a buy-only rule gets no credit just because the market rose. Also avoid look-ahead and include costs.
  4. In the book's test, 6,402 simple rules on the S&P 500 were tried, and none was significant after the correction.
