## Curriculum
- Today's item: **C04, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality"**, David H. Bailey and Marcos López de Prado. The PDF says Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014. The plan's reference matches.
- Opened: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf (the authors' version of 2014-07-31). SSRN (403) and the journal page (429) did not open.
- What it says, in simple words:
  1. If you test many strategies and keep the best one, its backtest looks better than it really is, even when none of them has an edge. The more you test, the better the luckiest one looks.
  2. The bar should therefore rise with the number of trials. It should also depend on how different the trials' results are, how long the test is, and the shape of the returns (skewness and kurtosis).
  3. Only *independent* trials should count. Trials that are near copies of each other count as less than one each.
- What it means for us:
  1. Our trials counter (`engine/trials.py`) uses a Bonferroni bar that counts every strategy version × timeframe as one trial (210 today, bar t >= 3.49).
  2. The retired VRVOL cards took exactly the same trades as donchian_breakout-VEXIT-S4 but still count, so our bar is stricter than the paper's.
  3. Our bar also ignores skew. That may make it too loose for strategies with rare large losses (mean reversion) and too strict for breakouts with rare large wins. This is my reading, not a measurement.
- Record: `[C04] The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey, Lopez de Prado 2014)` in `memory/research_sources.md`.
