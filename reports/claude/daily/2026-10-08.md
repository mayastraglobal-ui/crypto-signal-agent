## Curriculum
- Today's item: **C05, "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns"**, Campbell R. Harvey, Yan Liu, Heqing Zhu. Review of Financial Studies, volume 29, number 1, 2016. The plan's reference matches.
- Opened:
  - the published article on Harvey's Duke page: https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF
  - the NBER working paper page: https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592
  - The journal page was blocked (403).
- What it says, in simple words:
  1. Researchers have published 316 "factors" that supposedly explain stock returns. If you test that many ideas, some look good by pure luck.
  2. The usual rule (t-statistic above 2) is far too easy. A new factor should clear about **t = 3.0**. The authors think most published findings are probably false.
  3. There are two ways to correct for many tests:
     - **Bonferroni / Holm**: avoid even one false discovery. The bar keeps rising as more tests are run: 1.96 → 3.78 by 2012.
     - **BHY**: accept that a small share of discoveries is false. The bar levels off at 2.78 to 3.39.
  4. Unpublished failed tests also count. The true number of tests is always higher than the number you see.
- What it means for us:
  1. Our trials bar uses Bonferroni, the strictest choice: t >= 3.49 at 210 tests, and it rises with every new card.
  2. We already log every test, including the failures, which is the paper's main warning.
  3. Our best cell (donchian_breakout-VEXIT-S4@1.0 4h, t 4.799) clears all of the paper's bars. What blocks it is the drawdown gate, not the trials bar.
