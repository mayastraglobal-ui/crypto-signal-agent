## SMC/ICT concept
**The concept: key levels and the break of structure.** The ICT claim is that a close beyond the last swing (a "break of structure") shows the trend has changed or will continue, and that levels like the previous day's high and low are where price reacts.

**What evidence exists (critical view, source 2):**
1. Levels published by six foreign exchange firms "help to predict intraday trend interruptions". That is real evidence that some levels matter.
2. But the abstract says interruptions of a trend, not reversals, gives no hit rate, and says the power varies by exchange rate and by firm.
3. Our numbers: S8-PDH-PDL-SWEEP-noSMC 30m lost -0.36R over 855 trades earlier in October. This week S5-SWEEP-MSS-FVG-noSMC 15m (the version without the SMC ingredient) went from BACKTESTING to FAILED (average +0.05R, profit factor 1.10, not profitable in both train and unseen test), and S7-SILVER-BULLET-noSMC 15m went to FAILED (not cost-viable: fees and slippage 0.28R a trade). The versions with the ingredient have too few trades against their twins, so the ingredient is still unproven.

**Why retail traders fail with it:**
1. A break of structure is known only after the candle closes, so the entry is late (`late_entry` is systematic in 6 tests).
2. The level a trader draws is not the level professional firms publish.
3. A pause is not a direction, and the stop sits where the pause ends.

**What to do:** nothing by eye. The cards stay in testing against their twins. Record: `memory/smc_research.md`.

## How traders lose money
**Case study: the disposition effect (source 3).**
1. In the trading records of 10,000 accounts at a large discount broker, investors strongly prefer to sell winners over losers.
2. Rebalancing and trading costs do not explain it, and later performance does not justify it.
3. The page does not give the years or the proportions, and the journal and year are not shown on it (they come from a search result).
