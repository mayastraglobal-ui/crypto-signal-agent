## Strategy idea
**The gap:** RANGE is on BTC 1h, ETH 4h, ETH 1h and SOL 1h right now. The playbook says: "no tested strategy made money in this regime". Mean reversion lost -0.20R per trade over 12020 backtest trades there.

**Research finding (source 1, SOURCE → CLAIM):**
1. On 183 Binance pairs, 90% show significant reversal at 15 minutes. "Reversal" means the next candle tends to go the other way.
2. The gross edge is about 1.3 bp (basis points; 1 bp = 0.01%) per trade. The round-trip cost is 5 bp. The authors say it is "too small to clear" costs.
3. The reversal is strongest after moves driven by aggressive taker flow. A taker is someone who buys or sells at market instead of waiting.

**What it means for us:**
1. Our failures fit this finding. rsi2_dip_buy 15m lost -0.30R over 1736 trades and carries the fees_slippage tag. R4-BBRSI failed on both timeframes (1h -0.29R, 30m -0.32R).
2. So the idea "fade short moves" has a real effect behind it, but the effect is smaller than our costs.
3. **FORMAL DEFINITION → RULES:** two ways could get past the costs. One is to fade only very large stretches. R4-CLUC tests that: price must close 1.5% below the lower band. The other is to fade only moves pushed by aggressive flow. That is the new hypothesis `Hypothesis: taker-flow filter for mean reversion` in `memory/experiments.md`. It waits until the futures data has more history: 755 hours so far.
4. My honest expectation is weak. The evidence says small edges in this family usually die after costs. The engine decides.

## SMC/ICT concept
**The concept: the liquidity sweep (stop hunt).**
- **The ICT claim:** traders place stops just beyond obvious highs and lows, such as yesterday's high or low. "Smart money" pushes price through those stops to fill its own orders, then price reverses. This is the idea behind S8-PDH-PDL-SWEEP and liquidity_sweep_reversal.

**What evidence exists (critical view):**
1. **The first half is supported.** Osler (source 3) used real dealer order data in currency markets. Stop-loss orders do cluster, and they do move price.
2. **The second half is contradicted.** In the same study, trends *accelerate* when price reaches a stop cluster. Stop orders cause "self-reinforcing price movements". That is continuation, not reversal.
3. **Our BACKTEST numbers match the contradiction:**
   - S8-PDH-PDL-SWEEP 30m: -0.53R over 98 trades, and it does not beat its control twin.
   - S8 without the SMC part (noSMC) 30m: -0.45R over 518 trades.
   - liquidity_sweep_reversal loses on every timeframe: 5m -1.14R (374 trades), 15m -0.44R (471), 30m -0.51R (272) and 1h -0.24R (241).
   - The loss tag `sweep_continued` (price kept going after the sweep) is systematic in 2 tests.
4. No source I could open tests sweep reversals out of sample in crypto.

**Why retail traders fail with it:**
1. After the fact, every swing high looks like "liquidity". Before the fact, you cannot tell which sweep will reverse.
2. They fade the move while stop orders are still pushing price.
3. They put their own stop just beyond the sweep, where the next stop cluster sits.

**What to do:** do not trade sweeps by eye. The engine keeps S8 and the sweep cards in testing against their twins.

## How traders lose money
**Case study: popular indicator signals that lose after costs, plus over-leverage.**
1. **Oscillator signals (source 2).** The paper tested RSI, MACD and Bollinger Band rules with a strict three-step test on US stocks, and they failed it. The net-of-cost return gap after 10 bp costs was significantly negative: interval [-0.149, -0.044]. Trend rules were only "inconclusive".
2. **The same in crypto (source 1 and our BACKTEST numbers).** Short-term reversal exists, but the gross edge (1.3 bp) is below the cost (5 bp). Our oscillator-style cards failed the same way. R4-BBRSI 30m lost -0.32R over 1206 trades, and rsi2_dip_buy 15m lost -0.30R over 1736.
3. **Over-leverage (source 4).** On BitMEX, liquidated traders used about 60x leverage on average. Daily forced liquidations were 3.51% of long positions. At that leverage, the exchange closes the trade before the idea can play out.
4. **Testable rule:** fade a move only when aggressive taker flow drove it. On 1h, a long needs `taker_ratio` below a set level; a short is the mirror image. Compare with the parent card on the same period. Judge on unseen data and on the costs +50% test. It is written as `Hypothesis: taker-flow filter for mean reversion`. It waits for enough futures history to reach 30 or more unseen-data trades.
5. **Risk rule we already have:** risk is 0.5% of the account per trade, with an ATR stop, so the stop decides the leverage. For you: **never pick a position size by leverage.**

## Reference project
**R2: TradingAgents (bull vs bear debate, risk team).** Opened at release v0.5.1, commit 35543d0248bf89fcb92b17a15858ad0c0e940687 (the current main). I read the bull and bear researchers, the research manager (the debate judge), the three risk debaters, the portfolio manager and the debate-round settings. I also opened the paper's arXiv abstract (2412.20138, v7). Its return claims are the authors' CLAIM, and I did not check them.

**How it works:** language models argue bull vs bear (1 round by default), and a judge picks Buy / Overweight / Hold / Underweight / Sell. A trader writes a plan. Then aggressive, conservative and neutral risk debaters argue, and a portfolio manager makes the final call.

**What we take (ideas only):**
1. The judge is told: disagreement alone is not a reason to Hold, and arguments are weighed "independent of which side spoke first or last". For us, this means the Bull case and Bear case lines are read as equals, and "no trade" is chosen for thin or balanced evidence, not just because the lines disagree.
2. "Too thin to support a call" is a named reason. It matches our rule that too few trades = no verdict.

**What we deliberately do NOT take:**
- a language model making the trade decision or sizing it;
- the rating scale;
- a risk team that only argues (our Risk manager keeps a hard VETO from the engine's numbers);
- feeding past outcomes into prompts;
- any code (none was copied).
