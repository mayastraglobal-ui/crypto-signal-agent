## SMC/ICT concept
**The concept: the order block.**
- **The ICT claim:** the last opposite candle before a strong move marks where "institutions" placed orders. Price comes back to it and reacts in the original direction. S6-OB-FVG uses it.

**What evidence exists (critical view):**
1. **Levels and resting orders do line up.** Source 3 (US stocks) finds that support and resistance levels coincide with peaks in limit-order depth.
2. **But the rules only find orders that are already there.** The paper says the link comes from technical rules "locating depth already in place". That is a place where price may slow down, not a forecast of direction.
3. **A level can work both ways (source 1).** Take-profit orders at the level make price turn; stop orders just beyond it make price speed up. The level alone does not say which will happen.
4. **Our BACKTEST numbers cannot judge it yet:**
   - S6-OB-FVG 15m: 0 trades.
   - Its twin without SMC (S6-OB-FVG-noSMC 15m): FAILED, 94 trades, -0.159R.
   - S5-SWEEP-MSS-FVG: 12 trades at -0.839R (15m) and 9 at -0.653R (30m). Its twin: 29 trades at +0.074R and 13 at -0.023R.

**Why retail traders fail with it:**
1. Order blocks are drawn after the move, so every chart has one.
2. They put the stop just beyond the block, exactly where source 1 finds stop orders cluster.
3. They read a level as a direction.

**What to do:** do not trade order blocks by eye. The engine keeps them in testing against their twins.

## How traders lose money
**Case study: market orders (source 4).**
1. The study covers every investor in Taiwan.
