Crypto Futures Scalping Playbook
Complete rules for discretionary trading and for coding into a signal bot
Important: This is an educational framework, not financial advice. Scalping leveraged futures is high risk and most scalpers lose money, mainly through fees, overtrading and oversized leverage. Every number in this document is a starting point to be validated by your own backtests and journal, not a guarantee.



1. Philosophy and Structure
A professional scalper does not hunt for trades all day. They wait for a small number of pre-defined setups at pre-marked levels, during liquid sessions, with risk fixed before entry. Everything in this playbook follows one chain:
Context: Is today tradable? (news, session, funding, open interest, BTC direction)
Regime: Is the market trending or ranging on 1H?
Bias: Long, short or stand aside?
Location: Is price at one of my marked levels?
Setup: Which playbook strategy (A, B or C) matches this regime and location?
Trigger: Has the exact 5m entry condition closed?
Risk: Does the trade give at least 1.5R after fees, within my risk limits?
Management: Partial at 1R, breakeven, trail, time stop.
Review: Log it, measure expectancy, adjust only with data.
If any link in the chain fails, there is no trade. For the bot, each link becomes a filter function that returns pass or fail.
2. Pre-Session Routine
2.1 Economic calendar
Check high-impact US events: CPI, FOMC rate decision and press conference, NFP, PCE, GDP, major Fed speeches.
Rule: no new entries from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after a high-impact release. Close or protect open scalps before it.
Bot: maintain a news blackout table in UTC and set news_block = true inside those windows.

2.4 Levels to mark every day
Previous day high (PDH) and previous day low (PDL).
Weekly open, monthly open, previous week high and low.
Asia session high and low.
Previous day volume profile: POC, value area high (VAH), value area low (VAL).
Major 4H and 1H swing highs and lows (unbroken ones are strongest).
Round numbers (for BTC: every 1,000; for ETH: every 50 or 100).
Large liquidation clusters.
A "level zone" is the level plus or minus 0.25 x ATR(14) on 15m. Levels within one zone of each other merge into one stronger level. Trades are only taken inside or on a reaction from a level zone.
2.5 Coin selection
Trade only highly liquid perpetuals: BTC, ETH and a short list (for example SOL, BNB, XRP) that meet the filters below.
24h volume above a fixed threshold (for example over $500M on your exchange).
Average spread under 0.02% of price.
Maximum 2 to 4 pairs actively watched. Focus beats coverage.

4.2 Market structure definitions
Swing high (pivot): a candle whose high is higher than the highs of N candles on each side. Use N = 3 on 5m, N = 3 on 15m, N = 5 on 1H. A pivot is only confirmed N candles after it forms (no lookahead).
Swing low: mirror of swing high.
Bullish BOS (break of structure): a candle closes above the most recent confirmed swing high while in an up-sequence (higher highs and higher lows).
Bearish BOS: mirror.
CHoCH (change of character): the first close beyond a confirmed swing point against the current sequence. Bullish CHoCH = in a down-sequence, a close above the last lower high. Treated as a warning and as a precondition for reversal setups.
4.3 Candle and volume definitions
Term
Exact definition
Bullish rejection candle
Lower wick >= 60% of the candle range (high minus low), close in the top 33% of the range, range >= 0.6 x ATR(14)
Bearish rejection candle
Mirror: upper wick >= 60%, close in the bottom 33%
Bullish engulfing
Close > previous open, open <= previous close, body >= 1.2 x previous body, previous candle bearish
Volume spike
Candle volume >= 1.5 x the 20-period average volume
Strong breakout candle
Close beyond the level by >= 0.2 x ATR, body >= 60% of range, volume spike
Compression
On 15m: ATR(14) < 0.8 x its own 50-period average AND last 8 candles fit inside 1.2 x ATR(14)
Liquidity sweep
Wick trades beyond a level by at least 0.1 x ATR(5m), then a candle closes back inside the level within 3 candles
CVD divergence
Price makes a higher high (or lower low) versus the previous swing while CVD makes a lower high (or higher low)
Absorption
Strong negative delta candle at support that closes in its upper half (sellers hit the bid but price did not fall), or the mirror at resistance

5. The Playbook: Three Strategies
Each strategy has a regime it belongs to. Running a strategy outside its regime is the most common reason a good strategy shows bad results.
5.1 Strategy A: Trend Pullback
Element
Long rules (short = mirror)
Regime
Trend, bias bullish
Location
15m price pulls back into the zone between EMA21 and VWAP, and that zone overlaps or sits within 0.5 x ATR(15m) of a marked level, POC or prior breakout level
Pullback quality
Pullback is at least 0.8 x ATR(15m) deep, on lower volume than the impulse that preceded it, and has not broken the last 15m higher low
Trigger (5m)
Bullish rejection or bullish engulfing candle closes back above EMA9, with volume >= 1.2 x average. Optional confirmation: CVD rising or bullish CVD divergence on the pullback low
Entry
Limit order at the 50% level of the trigger candle, or market on close if the candle is small (< 0.8 x ATR)
Stop
Below the pullback swing low minus 0.1 x ATR(5m), or 1.5 x ATR(5m) from entry, whichever is tighter but not less than 0.8 x ATR(5m)
Targets
TP1 = 1R (close 50%). TP2 = previous 15m swing high or next level, minimum 2R
Invalidation
A 15m close below the pullback low, or 1H bias flips to neutral
Time stop
If +0.5R is not reached within 6 x 5m candles, exit at market

5.2 Strategy B: Liquidity Sweep Reversal
Element
Long rules (short = mirror)
Regime
Range or transition. In a trend, only counter-trend sweeps at major levels (PDL, weekly level, Asia low) with half size
Location
Price approaches a marked low: Asia low, PDL, VAL, range low or an equal-lows cluster
Sweep
A wick trades below the level by >= 0.1 x ATR(5m), then a 5m candle closes back above the level within 3 candles
Confirmation (need 1 of 3)
Bullish CVD divergence at the sweep; absorption candle at the sweep; or OI drops >= 1% during the sweep (longs flushed). Bonus: funding negative or neutral
Entry
Market on the close back inside, or limit at the retest of the level from above
Stop
Below the sweep wick minus 0.1 x ATR(5m)
Targets
TP1 = 1R or VWAP (whichever first, close 50%). TP2 = range midpoint, POC or opposite side of the range
Invalidation
Any 5m close back below the swept level after entry
Time stop
If +0.5R is not reached within 4 x 5m candles, exit. Good sweeps react fast

5.3 Strategy C: Breakout and Retest
Element
Long rules (short = mirror)
Regime
Trend in the direction of the breakout, or compression at the edge of a range near a session open
Pre-condition
15m compression (see definitions) directly under a marked level
Breakout
5m strong breakout candle closes above the level with a volume spike. Do not enter on the breakout candle
Retest
Within 12 x 5m candles, price returns to the level zone (level plus or minus 0.25 x ATR) and prints a bullish rejection candle, with CVD not making a new low
Entry
Limit at the level zone or market on the rejection close
Stop
Back inside the range: below the retest low and at least 0.5 x ATR(5m) below the level
Targets
TP1 = 1R (close 50%). TP2 = measured move (range height added to breakout level) or next marked level
Invalidation
A 5m close back inside the range by more than 0.3 x ATR
Cancel
If no retest within 12 candles, cancel the setup. Do not chase

5.4 No-trade conditions (override everything)
Inside a news blackout window.
Price crossing VWAP repeatedly with no structure (more than 4 crosses in the last 24 x 15m candles) and no clean range edges.
Spread above 2x normal, or order book depth sharply reduced.
Trade does not give >= 1.5R to TP2 after estimated fees and slippage.
Stop distance would be below 0.5 x ATR(5m) (too tight, will be noise-stopped) or above 3 x ATR(5m) (not a scalp).
Daily loss limit, consecutive-loss limit or max-trade limit has been hit.
Altcoin setup that conflicts with the BTC 15m structure.
Funding extremely against your side (for example above +0.05% per 8h when you want to long a breakout).

6.2 Position sizing
Size comes from the stop distance, never from leverage. Leverage is only what the exchange requires to open the calculated size.
risk_amount   = equity * risk_pct                 # e.g. 1000 * 0.005 = 5 USDT
stop_distance = abs(entry - stop)                 # e.g. 60000 - 59850 = 150
fee_buffer    = entry * (fee_entry + fee_exit)    # cost per unit of the round trip
qty           = risk_amount / (stop_distance + fee_buffer)
notional      = qty * entry
leverage_used = ceil(notional / margin_allocated)  # informational only
Worked example: $1,000 equity, 0.5% risk = $5. Entry 60,000, stop 59,850 (150). Taker fee 0.05% each side = 60 per BTC round trip. qty = 5 / (150 + 60) = 0.0238 BTC, notional about $1,430. Notice how much the fee reduces your size: on tight scalps, fees are a large share of risk.
6.3 Fee and slippage control
Use limit (maker) orders for entries and TP1 whenever possible. Use market orders only for stops, invalidations and time stops.
Include fees and an estimated slippage (for example 0.01% to 0.02% per side on majors) in every R:R calculation and every backtest.
Use your exchange fee tier and any fee-token discounts; small fee differences change scalping profitability materially.
7. Trade Management
At entry: stop-loss order placed immediately on the exchange (never a mental stop). TP1 limit order placed.
At +1R (TP1): close 50%, move stop to breakeven plus fees.
Trailing the remainder: trail behind the most recent confirmed 5m swing low (long) or below EMA9 on 5m closes, whichever is further. In strong trend days, trail on 15m swings instead.
TP2: fixed at the next level or measured move. Close everything there.
Time stop: per strategy (4 to 6 x 5m candles to reach +0.5R).
Invalidation exit: exit immediately on the strategy invalidation condition, even if the stop is not hit.
Never: widen a stop, add to a losing position, or remove a stop before news.

8.2 Engineering rules (avoid fake backtest results)
Closed candles only. Evaluate signals only when a candle closes. Never use the forming candle for decisions.
No lookahead. Swing pivots are confirmed N bars late; your code must reflect that delay. Higher-timeframe values must use the last closed higher-timeframe candle only.
All times in UTC for sessions, news windows and daily resets.
Deduplication and cooldown: one alert per setup; after an alert on a symbol, cooldown of 3 x 5m candles for the same strategy and direction.
Data health checks: if a WebSocket disconnects or candles have gaps, pause signals until data is consistent.
State machine per setup: IDLE, then SETUP_FORMING, TRIGGERED, IN_TRADE, MANAGED, CLOSED or CANCELLED. This prevents duplicate and contradictory signals.
8.3 Signal pipeline
on_candle_close(symbol, tf="5m"):
    ctx = build_context(symbol)          # sessions, news, funding, OI, BTC state
    if not context_ok(ctx):  return     # news_block, spread, limits, session
    regime = detect_regime(symbol, "1h")  # trend / range / transition
    bias   = detect_bias(symbol, "1h")    # bull / bear / neutral
    for strat in allowed_strategies(regime, bias):
        setup = strat.check_setup(symbol, "15m", levels)
        if not setup:  continue
        trig = strat.check_trigger(symbol, "5m", setup)
        if not trig:  continue
        plan = strat.build_plan(trig)    # entry zone, stop, tp1, tp2
        plan.rr = rr_after_fees(plan)
        if plan.rr < 1.5 or not stop_size_ok(plan):  continue
        plan.qty = position_size(equity, risk_pct, plan)
        if risk_limits_ok(plan):
            send_alert(plan); set_cooldown(symbol, strat)
8.4 Example: Strategy A trigger logic
def check_trigger_A_long(c5, c15, setup):
    last = c5[-1]                                     # last CLOSED 5m candle
    rng  = last.high - last.low
    if rng < 0.6 * atr(c5, 14):            return None
    lower_wick = min(last.open, last.close) - last.low
    rejection  = lower_wick >= 0.6 * rng and last.close >= last.low + 0.67 * rng
    engulf     = is_bullish_engulfing(c5[-2], last)
    if not (rejection or engulf):          return None
    if last.close <= ema(c5, 9)[-1]:       return None
    if last.volume < 1.2 * sma(volume(c5), 20)[-1]:  return None
    if broke_last_higher_low(c15):         return None
    stop = setup.pullback_low - 0.1 * atr(c5, 14)
    return Trigger(entry=(last.high + last.low) / 2, stop=stop)

9. Testing and Validation
9.1 Process
Backtest each strategy separately on at least 6 to 12 months of 5m data per symbol, including fees, slippage and funding for any position held across a funding time.
Split data: tune on the first 60 to 70%, test untouched on the remaining 30 to 40% (out-of-sample).
Walk-forward: re-run the tuning on rolling windows (for example 3 months tune, 1 month test) to check stability.
Parameter robustness: results should stay positive when a parameter moves 10 to 20% (for example ATR multiple 1.3 or 1.7 instead of 1.5). If only one exact value works, it is overfitted.
Alert-only / paper phase: run live for 2 to 4 weeks or at least 50 signals, compare to backtest.
Live at minimum size for the next 50 to 100 trades before increasing risk toward normal.

9.3 Kill switch
If the rolling 30-trade expectancy of a strategy turns negative, switch that strategy to alert-only until it recovers in paper trading.
If live drawdown exceeds 1.5x the backtest maximum drawdown, stop all live trading and review.
10. Journal and Review
10.1 Log for every trade
Date and time (UTC), symbol, direction, strategy, regime, bias, session.
Entry, stop, TP1, TP2, exit price, result in R, fees paid, slippage.
Screenshot of 15m and 5m at entry.
Rule-follow score: did the trade match the playbook exactly (yes / no)? Any rule-break counts as an error even if it won.
Emotional state (calm, rushed, frustrated, overconfident).
10.2 Review schedule
Daily (5 minutes): were all limits respected? Any rule breaks?
Weekly: expectancy by strategy, session and symbol. Remove or reduce the weakest combination.
Monthly: compare live to backtest. Change only one parameter at a time, and only with data.
11. Psychology Rules
Trade only setups in this playbook. A trade outside the playbook is a mistake, even if it wins.
No trading when tired, ill, angry or distracted.
After a large win, the next trade is at half size.
Never increase size to win back a loss.
Accept missed trades. Missing a move costs nothing; chasing it often costs a loss.
Let the bot find and alert; let rules, not feelings, decide whether to take it.
